# Total War Early Warning: Indicator Framework

A reference for a personal dashboard that answers one question: **is a large-scale nuclear war becoming likely enough that I should act on my departure plan?**

The framework is built for a household based in Valbirse, Switzerland, with Donegal (Crolly / Carrickfinn) as the fallback destination. Most of it applies anywhere in Europe.

---

## 1. How to think about warning

Large wars rarely arrive without a build-up. The historical pattern is a ladder: political crisis, conventional fighting, direct contact between nuclear powers, nuclear signalling, limited use, then general war. Each rung usually takes days to weeks, and the dashboard's job is to show which rung we are on.

Three principles matter more than any single indicator:

1. **Convergence beats intensity.** One alarming headline means little. Several independent indicators moving together (rhetoric, force posture, diplomacy, logistics) is what preceded real crises.
2. **Actions beat words.** Russian nuclear rhetoric has been near-constant since 2022 and is partly routine. Movements of warheads, submarines, and leaders are much harder to fake and far more meaningful.
3. **Some paths give no warning.** False alarms, accidents, and very rapid escalation can skip the ladder entirely. The dashboard covers the slow scenarios, which are the only ones where leaving early is possible.

---

## 2. The escalation ladder

| Level | Situation | Typical lead time to general war | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal rivalry, no major war | Years | Nothing to do beyond basic preparedness |
| 1 | Major war involving one nuclear power (e.g. Russia–Ukraine), routine nuclear rhetoric | Years, possibly never | Keep plans current, passports valid |
| 2 | Serious crisis between nuclear powers: incidents, ultimatums, talks broken off | Months to weeks | Review plan, check logistics, set money aside |
| 3 | Direct conventional fighting between nuclear powers (NATO–Russia, US–China) | Weeks to days | **Primary trigger zone.** Leave while transport works normally |
| 4 | Nuclear signalling: forces on alert, warheads moved, explicit threats tied to specific events | Days to hours | Last realistic window to travel |
| 5 | Any nuclear use | Hours or none | Shelter in place. Do not travel |

The goal is to act at Level 3, or at Level 4 at the latest.

## 3. Indicator categories

Each category lists what to watch, why it matters, and how to read it as green, amber, or red.

### 3.1 Strategic context (slow-moving, checked monthly)

These set the background risk. They change slowly and should not trigger action on their own.

- **Arms control status.** Treaties, inspections, and data exchanges between the US, Russia, and China. New START expired in February 2026 with no replacement. Any new talks would be a positive signal; withdrawal from remaining agreements (e.g. the Comprehensive Test Ban) a negative one.
- **Nuclear testing.** A resumed explosive nuclear test by any of the major powers would be a major signal of deteriorating restraint.
- **Doctrine changes.** Russia revised its nuclear doctrine in November 2024 to widen the circumstances for use. Further lowering of thresholds, or similar moves by the US or China, raise background risk.
- **Arsenal growth and deployments.** China's build-up, Russian weapons stationed in Belarus, any new forward deployments in Europe.

*Reading:* Green if stable or improving. Amber if deteriorating. This category alone should never go red.

### 3.2 Conventional conflict (checked daily during crises)

This is the most important category. Almost every credible path to total war runs through a conventional war between nuclear powers.

- **Direct NATO–Russia combat:** strikes on NATO territory, aircraft or ships of either side shot down, troops in direct contact.
- **NATO treaty actions:** Article 4 consultations (amber), Article 5 invocation (red).
- **Flashpoints in Europe:** the Baltic states, the Suwałki gap between Poland and Lithuania, the Black Sea, Kaliningrad, the Arctic.
- **Asia:** a Chinese blockade or invasion of Taiwan, US–China naval or air clashes in the South China Sea.
- **South Asia:** India–Pakistan fighting, especially strikes deep into either country. A regional nuclear war there would not hit Switzerland directly but could trigger global climate and food effects.

*Reading:* Amber for serious incidents or Article 4. Red for sustained direct combat between nuclear powers or Article 5.

### 3.3 Nuclear force posture (the strongest signals)

Changes to how nuclear forces are deployed are hard to hide, costly to fake, and the clearest sign that leaders are preparing.

- **Ballistic missile submarines leaving port en masse.** Normally a fraction are at sea. A surge from Russia's Northern Fleet, or from Faslane in Scotland, is a major signal, often reported by open-source analysts.
- **Bomber dispersal.** Strategic bombers moving from main bases to dispersal airfields.
- **Warhead movements.** Russia stores its tactical warheads centrally. Reports of warheads moving to delivery units (rather than routine exercises) are one of the most serious possible indicators.
- **Alert levels.** Publicly announced changes (as Russia did in February 2022) or credible leaks of raised US readiness.
- **Unusual exercises.** Large nuclear exercises outside their normal schedule, or combined with a live crisis.
- **Airborne command posts.** US "doomsday" aircraft (E-4B, E-6B) and equivalent Russian aircraft can sometimes be tracked on public flight-tracking sites. Unusual numbers airborne at once is significant.

*Reading:* Amber for any single unusual posture change. Red for several at once, or any credible report of warheads moved to delivery units.

### 3.4 Political rhetoric (read carefully)

Rhetoric matters mainly when it changes in kind, not volume.

- **Routine (green to amber):** general reminders of nuclear capability, state TV threats, statements by secondary officials.
- **Significant (amber):** heads of state linking nuclear use to a specific event ("if X happens, we will...").
- **Serious (red):** ultimatums with deadlines, formal announcements of nuclear readiness, or a sudden silence and breakdown of normal communication after a period of threats.

### 3.5 Government continuity and civil defence (very strong signals)

Governments quietly prepare to survive before a war. These are among the hardest signals to fake.

- **Leaders relocating** to protected sites or disappearing from public view for unexplained periods.
- **Civil defence activation:** in Switzerland, shelter readiness checks, civil protection mobilisation, Alertswiss warnings. Elsewhere, public shelter openings or evacuation planning.
- **Iodine tablet distribution** beyond normal schedules.
- **Emergency powers or martial law** in NATO states.
- **Stockpile releases**, fuel rationing, or restrictions on private flying.

*Reading:* Any of these in a major NATO country or in Switzerland is red.

### 3.6 Diplomacy

- **Embassy drawdowns or evacuation advice** from Western embassies in Moscow, Beijing, or capitals near the front line.
- **Travel advisories:** Switzerland's Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (FDFA) and Ireland's Department of Foreign Affairs. Advice to leave a country, rather than avoid travel to it, is the stronger signal.
- **Hotlines and talks:** reports that the Washington–Moscow hotline has been used are a sign of a crisis and of management. Talks broken off entirely are worse.
- **Mass diplomatic expulsions** and closure of consulates.

### 3.7 Markets and economic stress (supporting signals)

Markets react quickly and reflect what professionals with better information expect. Use as confirmation, not as triggers.

- Gold, oil, and Swiss franc spikes beyond normal volatility.
- The VIX (equity volatility index) at crisis levels.
- War-risk insurance cancelled for shipping or aviation in specific regions.
- Airlines suspending routes or rerouting around wide areas.

### 3.8 Infrastructure and information warfare

- Cuts to undersea internet or power cables, particularly in the Baltic and North Sea.
- Large-scale cyberattacks on power grids, banks, or communications in NATO states.
- Widespread GPS jamming beyond the already common Baltic interference.
- Satellite interference or anti-satellite weapon use.

These often precede or accompany military operations. A sudden cluster is amber; attacks causing national outages are red.

### 3.9 Departure logistics (your personal window)

This category does not measure war risk. It measures whether your window to leave is closing.

- Seat availability and prices on Zurich, Basel, and Geneva flights to Dublin and Belfast.
- Ferry schedules and capacity on Cherbourg–Rosslare and Cherbourg–Dublin.
- Fuel availability and prices in Switzerland and France.
- Border status between Switzerland and France, and Ireland's arrival rules.
- Airspace closures and NOTAMs (relevant if flying privately).

*Reading:* Amber when prices spike or seats sell out. Red when routes are suspended. If this goes red before the risk categories, the window is closing faster than the threat is rising, and that matters on its own.

## 4. Combining the signals

A simple scoring approach that avoids overreacting to single events:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Nuclear force posture | High |
| Government continuity and civil defence | High |
| Conventional conflict | High |
| Diplomacy | Medium |
| Political rhetoric | Medium |
| Infrastructure and information warfare | Medium |
| Markets | Low |
| Strategic context | Low (background only) |

**Overall status rules:**

- **Green:** no high-weight category above green.
- **Amber:** any high-weight category amber, or two or more medium-weight categories amber.
- **Red:** any two high-weight categories red, or one high-weight red plus two others amber or worse.

Departure logistics are shown separately, alongside the overall status, so you can see both the threat and the window at a glance.

## 5. Personal triggers

Decide these now, while calm, and commit to acting on them without re-debating them at the time. Suggested starting points:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Overall status amber for more than a week | Review plan, confirm documents, check flights, move savings to accessible accounts |
| Direct sustained combat between NATO and Russia, or US and China | **Leave** |
| Credible reports of Russian tactical warheads moved to delivery units | **Leave** |
| Swiss civil defence mobilised or shelter checks ordered | **Leave** if transport is still normal, otherwise prepare to shelter |
| Western embassies advising citizens to leave Moscow or Beijing, alongside amber military indicators | **Leave** |
| Any nuclear detonation anywhere | **Shelter in place.** Do not travel |
| Shelter order from authorities | **Shelter in place.** Do not travel |

The hardest part is not spotting the triggers but acting on them. In every historical crisis, most people who could have left talked themselves out of it.

## 6. Lessons from past crises

- **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962):** a two-week crisis with very visible signals (naval blockade, US forces at DEFCON 2, public ultimatums). Under this framework it would have gone red. War was avoided partly through back-channel diplomacy and partly through luck, including a Soviet submarine officer who refused to approve a nuclear torpedo launch.
- **Able Archer (1983):** a NATO exercise that the Soviet leadership partly believed was cover for a real attack. Public signals were weak. This is the type of crisis a dashboard would largely miss.
- **Petrov incident (1983):** a false satellite warning of US missile launches. No public warning at all.
- **Norwegian rocket incident (1995):** a scientific rocket briefly mistaken for an attack by Russian early warning. Again, no public warning.
- **February 2022:** months of visible Russian build-up, US intelligence warnings, and a publicly announced Russian nuclear alert. This framework would have gone amber early and briefly red, without war between nuclear powers following. A useful reminder that red does not mean certain.

## 7. Data sources

| Source | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Metaculus, Good Judgment Open | Forecast probabilities for nuclear use and great-power war | Public, frequently updated. Watch for sudden jumps, not absolute values |
| Institute for the Study of War (ISW) | Daily assessments of Russia–Ukraine and wider escalation | Good for conventional conflict |
| Federation of American Scientists (Nuclear Notebook) | Arsenal sizes, deployments, posture | Slow-moving but authoritative |
| Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists | Strategic context, expert commentary | Annual Doomsday Clock is background only |
| IISS, SIPRI | Strategic context, military balance | Reference rather than live |
| ACLED, GDELT | Conflict and event data | ACLED requires registration; GDELT is free and noisy |
| FDFA (eda.admin.ch), Ireland DFA (ireland.ie) | Travel advisories | Official, and a strong signal when they change |
| Alertswiss | Swiss civil defence alerts | Install the app; it is the authoritative local warning channel |
| Flight-tracking sites (ADS-B Exchange, Flightradar24) | Airspace closures, unusual military flights | Military aircraft often hidden on commercial trackers |
| EUROCONTROL, NOTAM services | Airspace restrictions | Relevant for private flying |
| Ferry operators (Irish Ferries, Stena Line, Brittany Ferries) | Route status and capacity | Check schedules for Cherbourg and Roscoff routes |
| Market data (gold, oil, CHF, VIX) | Supporting stress signals | Confirmation only |
| Open-source analysts on social media | Submarine movements, bomber dispersal, satellite imagery | Fast but variable quality. Require two independent sources |

## 8. Baseline as of mid-2026

Based on information up to mid-2026, and to be updated from live sources:

- **Strategic context: amber.** New START has expired, China's arsenal is growing, and Russia has lowered its stated threshold for use.
- **Conventional conflict: amber.** The Russia–Ukraine war continues, with periodic incidents involving NATO airspace and infrastructure, but no sustained direct NATO–Russia combat.
- **Nuclear force posture: green to amber.** Routine exercises and rhetoric, no confirmed abnormal posture changes.
- **Government continuity: green.**
- **Overall: amber at Level 1 on the ladder.** Elevated background risk, no immediate signals.

## 9. Dashboard design notes

- **Always visible, rarely alarming.** One overall status, one logistics status, and the current ladder level at the top. Detail below, collapsed by default.
- **Update cadence:** strategic context monthly, everything else daily, with real-time alerts only when a personal trigger is hit.
- **Show trends, not just states.** A category moving from green to amber over a week matters more than a single reading.
- **Two-source rule.** No indicator moves to red on a single unverified report.
- **Record history.** Keeping a log of past amber and red periods shows how often the dashboard has flagged a crisis that passed, which helps calibrate trust in it.
- **Built to calm, not to feed anxiety.** No refreshing ticker, no breaking-news feed on the main view. The point is that you can check it once a day, see green or amber, and get on with your life.
- **Offline fallback.** Keep a printed copy of your triggers, routes, and contacts. If the dashboard goes dark, that is itself information.
